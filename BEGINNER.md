## Monday

### Shoulders & Back of Legs

**Main exercises**
- **Overhead press** · 3 × 10 — Sit or stand holding a dumbbell in each hand at shoulder height, then push them straight up overhead and lower slowly.
- **Hands-up-high push-up** · 3 × 6 — Put your hands on a bench or step, walk your feet in so your hips are high and your body makes an upside-down "V", then bend your elbows to lower your head toward the bench and push back up.
- **Weighted bow** · 3 × 10 — Hold dumbbells in front of your thighs, keep your back flat and knees slightly bent, and push your hips back to lower the weights to just below your knees, then stand up tall.
- **Hip lift** · 3 × 12 — No weights needed. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, squeeze your butt and lift your hips until your body is a straight line from knees to shoulders.

**Extras if you have time**
- **Side raises** · 2 × 12 — Light dumbbells at your sides, lift your arms out to shoulder height and lower slowly.
- **Band pull-apart** · 2 × 15 — Hold a band in front of you at chest height with straight arms and stretch it apart until it touches your chest.
- **Heel raises** · 2 × 15 — Stand on the edge of a step and rise up onto your toes.
- **Weighted walk** · 30 seconds — Carry a heavy dumbbell in each hand and walk, standing tall.

**Tip:** Learn the movement before adding weight. Slow and controlled beats heavy and wobbly every time.

## Tuesday

### Core & Grip

A short, easy day. These build the stomach and grip strength that make everything else easier later.

**Main exercises**
- **Hang from a bar** · 60 seconds total — Grab a pull-up bar and just hang. Split it into as many short hangs as you need.
- **Banana hold** · 3 × 20 seconds — Lie on your back, press your lower back into the floor, and lift your shoulders and legs a little off the ground. Bend your knees to make it easier.
- **Leg lifts** · 3 × 10 — Lie on your back, hands under your hips, and slowly lift your legs up and lower them back down. Bend your knees to make it easier.

**Extras if you have time**
- **Plank** · 3 × 30 seconds — Hold a push-up position on your forearms with your body straight.
- **Side plank** · 2 × 20 seconds per side — On one forearm and the side of your feet (or knees), keep your hips lifted.
- **Opposite arm and leg reach** · 2 × 10 — On hands and knees, reach one arm forward and the opposite leg back, hold a second, switch.
- **Shoulder shrug hang** · 2 × 8 — Hang from the bar and, without bending your elbows, pull your shoulders down away from your ears, then relax.

**Tip:** You should finish this day feeling *better* than when you started. If hanging hurts your hands, do shorter hangs more often.

## Wednesday

### Push & Pull

**Main exercises**
- **Push-ups** · 3 × 10 — Start with hands on a wall or kitchen counter. As you get stronger, use something lower (a bench, a step), then the floor.
- **Dumbbell chest press** · 3 × 10 — Lie on your back on the floor or a bench, push the dumbbells straight up from your chest, then lower slowly.
- **Rows** · 3 × 10 per arm — Put one knee and hand on a bench, hold a dumbbell in the other hand, and pull it up to your side, then lower it.
- **Table rows** · 3 × 8 — No weights needed. Lie under a sturdy table, grab the edge, and pull your chest up toward it with your body straight. Bend your knees to make it easier.

**Extras if you have time**
- **Back-of-arm press** · 2 × 12 — Hold one dumbbell with both hands overhead, bend your elbows to lower it behind your head, then straighten your arms.
- **Shoulder blade push-up** · 2 × 10 — In a push-up position, keep your arms straight and let your chest sink a little between your shoulders, then push back up.

**Tip:** Do at least as much pulling as pushing. A strong upper back keeps your shoulders healthy.

## Thursday

### Balance & Stretching

An easy, low-pressure day. Nothing here involves being upside down.

**Main exercises**
- **Wrist warm-up** · 2 minutes — On hands and knees, gently rock forward and back, then turn your hands to face your knees and repeat.
- **Plank shoulder taps** · 3 × 10 per side — In a push-up position, lift one hand to touch the opposite shoulder, then switch. Try not to let your hips sway. Put your knees down to make it easier.
- **Bear hold** · 3 × 20 seconds — On hands and knees, lift your knees an inch off the floor and hold.
- **Bear crawl** · 3 × 20 seconds — From the bear hold, crawl forward a few steps, then back.

**Extras if you have time**
- **One-leg balance** · 30 seconds per side — Stand on one foot. Close your eyes to make it harder.
- **Deep squat hold** · 3 × 20 seconds — Hold a doorframe and sink into a low squat, keep your heels down, and stay there.
- **Cat and cow stretch** · 10 slow rounds — On hands and knees, round your back up like a cat, then let your belly drop and look up.
- **Hip lift hold** · 20 seconds — The Monday hip lift, but hold at the top.

**Tip:** Handstands can come much later, and they're optional. This day is about feeling strong and steady with your weight on your hands.

## Friday

### Hanging, Rows & Legs

**Main exercises**
- **Hang from a bar** · 3 × 20–30 seconds — Grab the bar with hands a little wider than your shoulders and just hang, relaxed. Shorter hangs are fine — build up over the weeks.
- **Shoulder shrug hang** · 3 × 8 — Hang from the bar and, without bending your elbows, pull your shoulders down away from your ears, hold for a second, then relax. Small movement, big payoff.
- **Rows** · 3 × 10 per arm — Same as Wednesday: one knee and hand on a bench, pull a dumbbell up to your side.
- **Table rows** · 3 × 8 — Same as Wednesday, if you don't have dumbbells.

**Legs**
- **Squats** · 3 × 12 — Feet shoulder-width apart, sit back and down like you're sitting into a chair, then stand up. Use a real chair behind you if you need to. Hold a dumbbell at your chest to make it harder.

**Extras if you have time**
- **Step-back lunges** · 2 × 8 per leg — Hold onto something for balance, step one foot back and lower your back knee toward the floor, then step back up.
- **Heel raises** — Easy to add at the end.
- **Weighted walk** — Same as Monday, good for grip.
- **Knee raises** · 2 × 8 — Hang from the bar (or lie on your back) and bring your knees up toward your chest.
- **Slow lower** · 3 × 3 — Once 30-second hangs feel easy: stand on a chair so your chin is already over the bar, then step off and lower yourself as slowly as you can.

**Tip:** Hanging and shoulder shrugs are the foundation for your first pull-up. They build grip and teach your shoulders to do the work. Write down your hang times each week and watch them climb.

## Progression Notes

**How to read this**
- **3 × 10** means do the exercise 10 times, rest about a minute, and repeat until you've done 3 rounds
- **3 × 20 seconds** means hold the position for 20 seconds, 3 times
- Pick a weight or difficulty where the last few are hard but you could still do 1–2 more
- What you'll want: a pair of dumbbells, a pull-up bar, and a resistance band (a big stretchy loop). Swaps are given if you don't have them.

**When to make it harder**
- Once every round feels doable with good form, make it a little harder: a slightly heavier weight, a lower surface for push-ups, or a lighter band
- Change one thing at a time

**Simple weekly rule**
- Aim for the three strength days (Mon / Wed / Fri). Tuesday and Thursday are a bonus.
- Some muscle soreness the next day is normal. Sharp pain in a joint means stop and pick a different exercise.

**Ready for the standard program?**
- 10 push-ups from the floor, 3 rounds → try the standard Wednesday
- A few full pull-ups → try the standard Friday and Tuesday
- Bear holds and hands-up-high push-ups feel easy → try handstand practice against a wall on the standard Thursday
